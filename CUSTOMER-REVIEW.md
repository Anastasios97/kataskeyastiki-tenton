# Website review — from a potential customer's point of view

**Reviewed:** current `main` build (commit `b43f9bb`, "Redesign site and move photos to one folder per product"), run locally on desktop (1280px) and mobile (390px).
**Persona:** a homeowner in southern Athens looking for a new awning or pergola for their balcony, and a café owner thinking about a pergola for their terrace. Both found the site through Google, Facebook or Instagram.

---

## Verdict

**7 / 10. The site looks like a serious company and is easy to contact, but it doesn't yet show *me* enough proof of this company's own work.**

The first impression is excellent. It looks better than most Greek awning-company sites: the design is clean, the phone number is always visible, and the free-estimate offer is clear. It explains the products better than most competitors. As a customer I'd understand the difference between an antirida, folding arms and a cassette in a few minutes.

But when I try to decide *whether to trust this company with my balcony*, I keep finding gaps. Many of the best-looking images are clearly computer-generated, not this company's projects. Nobody else vouches for them: there are no reviews or testimonials. And the things I actually want to know aren't there: rough cost, warranty, how long it takes, and when I can call.

---

## What works well (keep it)

1. **Contacting you is easy.** On desktop the phone number and "Ζητήστε προσφορά" stay at the top of every page. On mobile there's a sticky **Κλήση / Προσφορά** bar at the bottom. I never had to look for a way to reach you.
2. **The hero message is clear.** "Σκιά που σχεδιάζεται για να ζείτε καλύτερα", plus "family business, 30 years, Michalis Papadam". I immediately know what you do and that a real person is responsible.
3. **The product explanations are really helpful.** The cards on the classic-systems page ("Ιδανικό για… / Κερδίζετε…") and the pergola types (sloped, flat 90°, curved, hanging, large spans, on an existing structure) answer the question "which one do I need?" better than most competitors' sites.
4. **The 3-question quiz is a nice touch.** I answered "balcony with wind / economy / classic" and got "Αντιρίδα" with a link to the page. It's quick and it works.
5. **The real project photos are convincing where they exist.** Antirides (5), folding arms (5), cassettes (5) and pergola awnings (6 real ones) are phone photos of real Athens balconies, sometimes with the ladder still in the shot. That's exactly what builds trust. The lightbox with swipe and arrows works well.
6. **The fabric section shows choice.** Named suppliers (TenCate, DAS, Calbari), with codes and the line "the final choice is made with a physical sample". It tells me you'll bring samples, which is reassuring.
7. **The contact form is short and in plain language.** It has a spam honeypot and an email fallback if sending fails. The form is repeated at the bottom of the home page, so I don't have to go looking for it.

## What made me hesitate (fix these first)

### 1. The images are AI renders shown as "your work"
This is the biggest trust problem. A lot of the large, polished images are clearly computer-generated:

- the home hero and the same dusk villa repeated on the Pergkotentes and Bioklimatikes pages;
- the umbrellas, the car shelter, the vertical screens, the kapotina, the monoblock café;
- the "worker with a spirit level" on the company section;
- the three "Εφαρμογές" cards.

Some customers will notice, especially next to the real phone photos. Once I notice that one photo is fake, I start doubting the rest.

Where it hurts most:
- **The Bioklimatikes pergoles page** has exactly **one** photo, and it's a render, under the heading **"Φωτογραφίες έργων"**. As a customer about to spend thousands of euros on a bioclimatic pergola, I come away thinking "they've never built one".
- **The Pergkotentes gallery** opens with the render as the big first image, before the six real installations.
- **"Έργα μας" (29 photos)** mixes renders into the "real work" grid. Umbrellas, car protection, kapotines, vertical systems, monoblock and bioclimatic each have only a single image, and it's a render.
- **The home "Εφαρμογές" section openly says** *"Οι εικόνες είναι ενδεικτικές… Σύντομα η ενότητα θα εμπλουτιστεί με πραγματικά έργα."* "Coming soon" text on a live site reads as unfinished.

**Recommendation:** Keep renders only in hero banners, if you want them. Galleries, "Έργα μας" and anything labelled "Φωτογραφίες έργων" should show only real installations. Hide categories that have no real photos yet (the code already hides empty albums). Remove the "coming soon" sentence. Even 2–3 honest phone photos per category beat one beautiful render. A real photo of Michalis or the team on a job would do more for the company section than the AI worker.

### 2. No reviews or testimonials at all
There isn't a single customer quote, Google rating or star anywhere on the site. "Πάνω από 30 χρόνια εμπειρίας" is a claim; reviews are proof. Thirty years of customers should mean plenty of happy ones.

**Recommendation:** Add 3–6 short testimonials with first name and area (e.g. "Γιώργος, Άγιος Δημήτριος — περγκοτέντα"), ideally next to a photo of that job. Link to your Google Business Profile reviews. If you don't have a Google Business Profile, create one: that's where local customers look first.

### 3. Questions I wanted answered but couldn't find
| Question a customer actually has | Current site |
|---|---|
| Roughly how much will it cost? | Nothing. Not even "from €X" or a price range per system. |
| What warranty do I get? On the fabric, the motor, the installation? | Not mentioned anywhere. |
| How long from the visit to installation? | Not mentioned. |
| Is the visit/measurement really free, and in which areas? | "Δωρεάν εκτίμηση" is in the buttons, but the conditions aren't stated. |
| When can I call? | No opening hours. |
| Which of the 3 numbers do I call? | Three numbers are listed with no labels (mobile/office? who answers?). |
| Can I just send photos of my balcony on Viber/WhatsApp? | No, and the form has no photo upload. In Greece this is how many people prefer to start. |
| Where exactly are you? | There's an address link, but no map or showroom/workshop information. |

**Recommendation:** Add a short "Πώς δουλεύουμε" block with the steps and *typical* timings, a warranty line, a "from €…" or "typical range" per system, opening hours, labels on the phone numbers, and Viber/WhatsApp links.

### 4. Small things that chip away at trust
- **Grammar on the company page:** "με υπεύθυνο τον **Μιχάλης** Παπαδάμ" should be "τον **Μιχάλη** Παπαδάμ" (accusative). Greek customers will notice.
- **"Κλασσικά" vs "κλασικά":** standard modern spelling is *κλασικά*. It's used 14 times, including the main menu.
- **30 vs 30+:** the home badge says "30 χρόνια", the company page says "30+". Pick one. A founding year ("από το 1994") is more convincing than either.
- **The email is `k.tenton@yahoo.gr` and the address is `anastasios97.github.io/…`.** Both look like a personal project rather than an established company. A domain (e.g. `kataskevastiki-tenton.gr`) with a matching email is one of the cheapest credibility upgrades.
- **No legal/business details:** there's no privacy policy (the form collects names and phone numbers, so GDPR applies), no consent line linking to it, and no business registration details (ΑΦΜ / ΓΕΜΗ) in the footer.
- **"Τεχνικές πηγές" sections link out to LAMDA, MetaForm, NYFAN, Somfy and others.** As a customer I click, leave your site, and may end up finding another installer through the manufacturer. The supplier names do build trust, so keep them, but make them text or logos rather than prominent "Επισκεφθείτε την ιστοσελίδα" cards.
- **Fabric swatch images are loaded directly from supplier websites** (nyfan.gr, wixstatic.com). In my test environment, where those hosts were blocked, they showed as empty grey boxes. If a supplier renames or removes a file, the same happens to real visitors. Download the images (with permission) and serve them from your own site.
- **Pergola-type cards all use the same placeholder icon** (three dashes in a box), so that section looks unfinished. A small real photo or a simple diagram per type would help a lot.
- **The "UV" tile** in the pergola dimensions strip ("15 m / 10 m / 130 m² / UV") isn't a number like the others and reads oddly.
- **The antirida page hero** uses a side-by-side collage of two photos, so the banner looks like two half-images.

### 5. Mobile
Mobile works well overall: nothing overflows horizontally, and the menu and sticky call bar work. A few issues:
- The **home page is ~16,000px tall on a phone** (about 19 screens). The quiz alone takes 3–4 screens because its 12 option cards stack. Collapse the quiz on mobile or show one question at a time.
- **The hero stat strip ("30+ / Στα μέτρα / Ποιότητα") is cut off** at the bottom of the first screen, behind the sticky bar.
- **In the open menu, the second (yellow) button is hidden** behind the sticky Κλήση/Προσφορά bar.

### 6. Could customers even find these pages? (SEO)
The site is a single-page app on GitHub Pages. Inner pages such as `/pergkotentes` or `/klassika-systimata-skiasis/antirida` are served through `404.html`, so **search engines get an HTTP 404 status for every page except the home page**, even though they're listed in `sitemap.xml`. Someone searching "περγκοτέντα Αγιος Δημήτριος" is unlikely to land on the pergola page.

**Recommendation:** Generate a real `index.html` per route at build time (pre-rendering, or at least copying `index.html` into each route folder). Moving to a custom domain would help too.

### 7. The contact form: please verify it really delivers
The form posts to `formsubmit.co/ajax/k.tenton@yahoo.gr`. FormSubmit only delivers to an email address after a one-time **activation email** has been confirmed, and it can end up in Yahoo's spam folder. Send a test request from the live site and confirm it arrives. A form that silently loses leads is the most expensive bug a business site can have.
